When I joined as Lead DevOps & Compliance Engineer, the cloud bill was the
single largest line item nobody fully understood. Over a few months I brought it
down by roughly **80%**, without a single user-facing regression. Here's how.

## 1. Make the bill legible before touching anything

You can't optimise what you can't see. The first week was pure measurement:
cost allocation tags, per-service and per-environment breakdowns, and a simple
rule, every dollar must map to a team and a workload.

The biggest surprises are almost always the boring ones: idle non-prod
environments running 24/7, oversized instances chosen "to be safe," and
forgotten snapshots/volumes quietly accruing cost.

## 2. Right-size with evidence, not vibes

I pulled real CPU/memory/IO utilisation over 30 days and right-sized against the
**p95**, not the peak. Most fleets were provisioned 3–5x over what they used.

## 3. Let autoscaling do the night shift

Non-production environments now scale to zero outside working hours. Production
scales horizontally on real signals (queue depth, request latency) instead of
running a fixed, always-on peak fleet.

## 4. Architecture changes that move the needle

- Moved bursty, stateless work to spot/preemptible capacity with graceful drain.
- Added caching and CDN offload so origin compute did less work per request.
- Consolidated over-provisioned databases and tuned storage tiers.

## Takeaway

Cost optimisation isn't a one-time cleanup, it's an architecture discipline.
Tag everything, size against p95, scale to zero where you can, and review the
bill like you review latency.

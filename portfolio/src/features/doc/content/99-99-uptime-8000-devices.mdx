Four nines means roughly **under an hour of downtime a year**. Delivering that
across a multi-region fleet of **8,000+ devices**, while cutting infra cost by
50%, came down to a few principles applied relentlessly.

## Design around failure domains

Every dependency is a potential outage. I mapped the blast radius of each
component and made sure no single region, AZ, or service could take the whole
system down. Multi-region wasn't a marketing checkbox, it was the failure model.

## Make health observable, then automatic

- Real health checks (not just "is the port open").
- Automated failover and self-healing for the common failure modes.
- Dashboards and alerts tuned to symptoms users feel, not raw metrics noise.

## Roll out changes safely

Progressive delivery, canaries and staged rollouts, so a bad change hits 1% of
devices, not 100%. Fast, automated rollback when the canary looks wrong.

## Cost and reliability are the same conversation

Reliability often *saves* money: autoscaling to real demand, killing idle
capacity, and consolidating duplicated infrastructure took 50% off the bill
while making the system more predictable.

## Takeaway

Uptime isn't luck. It's failure-domain design, honest health signals, automated
recovery, and rollouts that fail small.
